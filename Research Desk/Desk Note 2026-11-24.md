## Brain alerts explained

**🔴 "day trader has trades with no recorded exit: SPY 2026-11-24"**

Root cause, from the code (`bot/daytrade_live.py`) and the job logs:

1. `flatten()` sends `DELETE /v2/orders` (cancel everything) and then, straight away, `DELETE /v2/positions?cancel_orders=true`. Alpaca cancels orders asynchronously. While the bracket's take-profit and stop legs are still "pending cancel", they hold the shares, so closing that symbol can be rejected inside the response. Alpaca answers the positions call with a multi-status body (one status per symbol). `Alpaca.call()` only raises on HTTP errors, and the body is never inspected. Then `flatten()` always prints "flat: all orders cancelled, all positions closed".
2. Evidence that this happens:
   - On **23 Nov** the log says "flat" at 20:55 UTC, but SPY 39 and QQQ 35 were still there the next morning. Today's 09:37 log line reads "left-over positions from an earlier session: QQQ 35, SPY 39 - closing them first", and the closing fills landed at 14:37 UTC on 24 Nov.
   - **18 Nov SPY** was also carried overnight.
   - In the morning the same function *does* work, because the brackets were already cancelled the evening before. Every time a position was still open at 15:55, it carried.
3. Today, SPY (84 shares long from 718.91, stop 712.16) was still open at 15:55. The report ran in the **same second** as the flatten (both at 20:55:04), found no SPY sell fill, and recorded the trade as "open". The fix in commit 17eb68d only settles carried trades from *earlier* days, so it can't clear today's.
4. **Is SPY open overnight?** Most likely yes, and it is unprotected, because the stop was cancelled. I can't confirm this from here because this desk has no broker access. Accounting check: the account is -$1,200.21 since 17 Nov, and recorded closed P&L is -$1,370.19. The +$169.98 gap matches 84 SPY shares marked about $2 above entry (around 720.9) at 15:55. A 2% gap tomorrow would be about ±$1,200 (about 2.4R) on a position meant to risk $494. Tomorrow's morning job will close it at the open (the left-over check), which is how 23 Nov's QQQ ended at **-1.38R / -$680**.

**🟠 "US stocks volatility 31% (1 month) vs 15% normal":** a real signal, not a bug. Monday was the best day since April 2025 (Nvidia +31% on the CEO news), and chips gave a lot back today. VIX is about 27. The bot's planned portfolio volatility (6.3%) is well under the cap. Today's realised 12.9% is still under the 22.3% cap.

**🟠 "real estate is within 0.3% of entering":** also real. VNQ's average 3/6/12-month return is +1.9% against T-bills at +2.2%. Nothing is broken. It is just close to the line, so the 1 Dec preview could still change.

## One idea worth testing

**One combined bet per day on SPY and QQQ.** Either trade only the first breakout, or give both together a single $500 risk.

The reason: they rarely give independent results. I ran a quick test on the repo's own data (no live rules changed). On the previous month's replay (`state/daytrade_month.json`, 44 ORB trades, 16 Oct–16 Nov), **20 of 22 days had SPY and QQQ in the same direction, and 18 of those 20 pairs ended the same way**.

| Rule | Month replay, total R | Live 17–24 Nov, total R |
|---|---|---|
| Both trades full size (old rule) | -1.64 | -1.91 |
| Second same-direction trade at half (current rule) | -1.52 | -0.75 |
| Skip the second same-direction trade | **-1.39** | **+0.41** |

*R summed per trade, with no compounding. Live includes today's SPY at 0R because its exit is unknown.*

The improvement is small and the samples are tiny (22 + 6 days), so this is a hypothesis, not a result. A proper test is to run `bot/daytrade_month.py` over 6–12 months of 5-minute bars with a "first breakout only" switch, then compare total R, worst day and win rate. It needs the Alpaca data keys, so it has to run in GitHub Actions, not here.

## Needs the owner

These are code and schedule fixes the desk is not allowed to make. Nothing has been changed.

1. **Make the flatten real** (`bot/daytrade_live.py`, `flatten()`):
   - Cancel the orders, then poll `GET /v2/orders?status=open` until it is empty (wait up to about 15 s).
   - Then close the positions and **read the multi-status body**: any symbol whose status isn't 200 is a failure.
   - Poll `GET /v2/positions` until it is empty. Retry the close once or twice, or send a plain market order per symbol.
   - Only log "flat" when the account really has no positions. If it isn't flat by 15:58, exit non-zero so GitHub emails you.
2. **Report after the fills arrive:** in `report()`, wait a few seconds after the flatten and re-read today's FILL activities until every entry has its exit. Otherwise today's trades will keep showing as "open".
3. **Close-job timing is fragile:** the close job only works if GitHub starts it inside a roughly 7-minute window (15:52–15:59 NY). Today the intended 20:52 UTC trigger arrived 48 minutes late and would have missed completely. The job only ran because the summer twin was 41 minutes late. Options:
   - Start the close job much earlier, for example at about 15:00 NY, and let the script sleep until 15:55. This costs more Actions minutes.
   - Trigger `workflow_dispatch` from an external scheduler.
   - Either way, keep a broker-side safety net so a position is never left without a stop.
4. **Right now:** run the day-trader workflow in "check" mode, or look in the Alpaca dashboard, to see whether SPY 84 is still open tonight. Decide yourself whether to close it before tomorrow's open. The desk does not touch orders.
5. **Smaller items:**
   - Entry slippage pushed SPY's real risk to $567 against the $500 limit. Consider sizing from the expected fill plus a buffer.
   - The daily long-term run's "equity" is a late-morning mark, because of the delays, not a close. That is worth labelling in [[10 - Live Paper Account]].
