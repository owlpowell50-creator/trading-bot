## Brain alerts explained

**🟠 "real estate is within 0.4% of leaving the portfolio"**
- This alert is partly caused by stale data. At 11:10 VNQ was 94.86. It closed at **95.68 (+0.95%)**, so the real cushion is **1.3%**.
- The alert would still show, because the Brain flags anything within 2%, but it is much less urgent.
- **Root cause:** `daily.yml` runs at 10:05 NY (later when GitHub is late). It writes today's row into `data/history.csv` with an intraday price. The close job then reruns `brain.think()` on that same stale file (`bot/brain.py` → `data.build_series()`).
- The row is corrected the next morning. That is why the rebalance itself, which uses the month-end close fetched on 1 Dec, is not affected. Only the "today" views are affected.

**🟠 "US stocks volatility 32% (1 month) vs 15% normal"**
- This is a real signal. Over five days the market went from a record Monday rally, to a chip sell-off, to a 3% intraday drop and recovery today. VIX is 28.
- The 32% is slightly too high, because the last data point is the -2.9% midday mark, not the -0.3% close.
- Actual portfolio volatility (about 13%) is well under the 22.3% cap.

**Yesterday's 🔴 "no recorded exit: SPY 2026-11-24" has cleared.** That is because the trade was settled this morning at -$1,777.44, not because the bug was fixed.

**Two smaller reporting bugs found today:**
1. **Target hits are labelled "closed before stop or target".**
   - Both of today's trades filled at their take-profit price: SPY target 714.53, filled 714.55; QQQ target 631.86, filled 632.00.
   - `_exit_kind()` in `bot/daytrade_live.py` only says "hit target" when R ≥ 1.8.
   - By design the target sits at 1.5 × the range height from the breakout close, while the stop is at the far side of the range. So a target hit is always about 1.45–1.5R, and the label can never say "hit target".
2. **The equity curve in [[10 - Live Paper Account]] is shifted by one day.**
   - Alpaca's daily points are stamped 01:00 UTC, which is 20:00 New York on the *previous* trading day. `bot/live.py` (line 92) takes the UTC date, so every close is labelled one day late.
   - That is why there is a "2026-11-21" row, which is a Saturday. The row labelled 25 Nov ($94,627) is really Tuesday's close.

## One idea worth testing

**Add a buffer around the T-bill hurdle in the long-term strategy.**
- A held asset stays in while its score is above *T-bills − x*. A new asset gets in only if it beats *T-bills + x*.
- Why test it: this month real estate is hovering right at the line, so a few tenths of a percent decide a 36% position. Edge cases like that cause whipsaw trades.

I ran it with the repo's own backtester (`bot/backtest.py`, live settings: 10 bp costs, 10% no-trade band, before tax) in a scratch copy. No live rules or files were changed.

| Variant (1997 → today) | CAGR | Sharpe | Max drawdown | Worst year | Rebalances that traded | Turnover/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Live CORE (no buffer) | 9.77% | 0.74 | -17.6% | -12.2% | 166 | 2.27 |
| Buffer ±0.5% | 9.76% | 0.73 | -19.0% | -13.5% | 167 | 2.22 |
| Buffer ±1% | 10.08% | 0.76 | -19.0% | -13.5% | 163 | 2.12 |
| Buffer ±2% | 10.03% | 0.76 | -17.8% | -12.5% | 160 | 2.05 |

- **±2% is the most interesting setting:** +0.26%/yr, about 10% less turnover, and almost the same worst drawdown. The gain shows up in both halves of the history (1997–2011: 12.0% vs 11.6%; 2012–2026: 8.1% vs 8.0%).
- Turnover matters most in a taxable account, so the after-tax gap could be bigger.
- **Caveats:** the gain is small, I picked the best of 3 buffer sizes, and there is only one history. Treat it as a lead, not a result.
- **What it would change on 1 Dec:** with ±2%, real estate (score 3.5% against the 4.2% it would need) would **not** enter. The account would move to about **30% commodities and 70% cash** instead of adding a 36% REIT position.
- **Next step for a proper test:** a walk-forward check (choose the buffer on 1997–2011 and judge it on 2012–2026), plus the after-tax numbers from `research.evaluate`.
